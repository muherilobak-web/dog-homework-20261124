## The excuse

> I ate your homework so the teacher never sees how wrong the answers were — no paper to mark means no bad grade, and you owe me a treat.

## Why this excuse

The dog in this line is not sorry at all. It believes it performed a rescue: by eating the homework it removed the evidence before the teacher could see the wrong answers, so the bad grade can never happen. That is the twist — the crime is presented as an act of loyalty, and the dog reports it proudly, the way a dog brings you something it is pleased with. The dog's priorities stay dog-shaped all the way through: no embarrassment, no apology, just the firm belief that destroying the bad thing equals helping, capped by a demand for a treat. Nothing in the line needs a second sentence to land, and it steers clear of the stock shapes — no blaming the cat, no praising the taste of paper, no claiming to be a good boy. The dog is the hero of its own crime, and it expects payment.
